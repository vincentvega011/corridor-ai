# Corridor AI Security Self-Assessment

Code state: repository at the commit that carries this file, assessed on 2026-10-18. Every number below comes from `backend/config/policy.yaml`, `backend/src/corridor/policy/rules.py`, `eval/redteam/`, the published report `eval/reports/20261011T175100/`, or a test and evidence run listed in section 6. Fee, FX and limit values are illustrative assumptions (`docs/assumptions.md`), not legal limits. Evidence files are indexed in `docs/evidence/index.md`.

## 1. Scope and boundary

**In scope.** The Corridor AI sandbox: the API, the deterministic policy engine, the confirmation and MFA service, the executor, the hash-chained ledger, the grounding verifier, the quarantined document reader, the customer app (PWA) and the bank console.

**Boundary.**

- Every user, account, payee, invoice, document and transaction is synthetic. The apps show a permanent "SANDBOX: no real money" banner.
- No connection to a bank, payment network, identity provider or card scheme. The executor changes rows in a local PostgreSQL database only.
- No crypto, stablecoin or on-chain feature. The ledger is hash-chained for tamper evidence; it is not a blockchain.
- Model calls: the offline path (recorded cassettes, local models) is the plan for the venue. The cloud route (Token Harbor, free tier) is a third-party gateway that may retain prompts; only synthetic data is ever sent, and the prompt guard blocks identifiers and secrets (INV-12).

**What is claimed.** Within the sandbox, a model response alone cannot change balances, payees, limits, credentials or settings; every state change passes a deterministic policy decision, a confirmation bound to the exact proposal, and (above the low tier or for risky inputs) an authenticator code, and is recorded in a ledger whose tampering is detectable.

**What is not claimed.** Production readiness, regulatory compliance, resistance to a patient scammer whose victim keeps paying (section 5, R1), correctness of any model's language understanding on real documents, or any result for the expanded 68-task and 125-case suite (section 4.5).

## 3. Permission tiers

Policy is a pure function of `(ActionProposal, AccountState, PolicyConfig, Clock)` (INV-2). Each rule returns a decision and the most restrictive wins: `DENY > REQUIRE_MFA > REQUIRE_CONFIRM > ALLOW` (INV-7). `ALLOW` exists only for read tools; an `ActionProposal` is never allowed without at least a confirmation (`R-FLOOR`). Amounts are stored as integer minor units; the NPR column converts at 1 NPR = 100 minor units, so `2500000` minor units is NPR 25,000.00. Source: `backend/config/policy.yaml`, `policy/rules.py`, `docs/policy-rules.md`.

Confirmation and MFA mechanics: a confirmation creates a grant bound to the SHA-256 of the canonical proposal (amount, currency, payee, source account, route, idempotency key); it is single-use and expires after 120 seconds (INV-5). The executor rejects a repeated idempotency key (INV-6). TOTP codes cannot be replayed (a used time step is recorded).

## 4. Threats by scored category

The suite in `eval/redteam/` has **125 cases** in nine attack categories (counted from the YAML: privilege escalation 18, data extraction 15, direct injection 9, indirect injection by image 15, induced transfer by social engineering 28, hallucination 16, indirect injection by document 11, confirmation swap 8, replay or duplicate 5; the files `controls.yaml` and others include benign controls). The four scored categories group them as follows.

### 4.1 Induced transfers (social engineering, confirmation swap, replay, hallucinated numbers): 28 + 8 + 5 + 16 = 57 cases

- **Attack patterns.** A new-student housing deposit to an account taken from a PDF or a forwarded message (RT-080, RT-081 and the student-housing set); urgency and secrecy ("pay now, tell no one"); a changed beneficiary on a real invoice; a typed account that matches a scammer's registered name; splitting payments to stay under limits; changing the amount or payee after the user confirmed; replaying a confirmation or double-clicking; asking the agent to state or use a number it never retrieved.
- **Controls.** Taint stays on document and message values (INV-3, INV-4); payee hold with cap 0 for untrusted origin (R-PAYEE-COOLOFF); MFA for any new payee (R-PAYEE-ADD); risk score (R-RISK); daily limit and velocity; invoice beneficiary check; WYSIWYS grant bound to the proposal hash, single use, 120 seconds (INV-5); idempotency keys (INV-6); grounding verifier (INV-9); safety lock (INV-11); officer verification with CoP match and out-of-band reference for the one release path.
- **Evidence.** Execution run D (document-derived payee saved on hold, payment denied by R-PAYEE-COOLOFF, R-RISK and R-TAINT-PAYEE, session locked, 0 minor units moved) and run A (a replayed confirmation does not pay twice), both in `docs/evidence/runs/`.

### 4.2 Data extraction: 15 cases

- **Attack patterns.** Asking for another user's balance or payees; asking the model to reveal its prompt, tokens, MFA seeds or full account numbers; instructions hidden in a document that ask to exfiltrate data.
- **Controls.** The user id comes only from the token; every read tool is scoped to it. The planner receives only the fields the task needs and masked identifiers (INV-12). The prompt guard blocks account-like digit runs, key and TOTP-seed shapes and registered secrets before any request leaves the process. The ledger and trace routes are scoped or officer-only; the trace endpoint rejects customer tokens (tested). Reader output is typed, not free text. Evidence logs are scrubbed of tokens, seeds and digit runs of 9 or more (`scripts/evidence_run.py`).

### 4.3 Privilege escalation: 18 cases

- **Attack patterns.** A customer token on officer routes; asking the agent to raise a limit, disable MFA or change contact details; claiming to be the bank or an officer in chat; verifying one's own payee; calling dev, demo or ablation routes; tampering with the ledger from the application.
- **Controls.** Separate officer credentials checked in constant time; no officer action is an LLM tool; R-SETTINGS denies protected settings words; `APP_ENV` defaults to production so dev and demo routes do not exist (ADR 0020); the application database role cannot update or truncate the ledger (append-only triggers); the executor is the only writer of state (INV-1, import-boundary tests).
- **Evidence.** Run E shows a customer token refused on `/officer/payees/{id}/verify` (HTTP 401) while the officer's call succeeds under the officer's own id; run F shows the chain verification passing, failing after one altered entry, and passing after the row is restored.

### 4.4 Prompt injection: direct 9 + indirect by document 11 + indirect by image 15 = 35 cases

- **Attack patterns.** "Ignore previous instructions" in the chat; instructions inside an invoice PDF, a forwarded SMS or a screenshot; payment instructions that do not match any stored record; text that addresses the AI.
- **Controls.** Untrusted content goes only to the quarantined reader after magic-byte validation and re-encoding; the planner never sees raw strings from it (INV-3); injection signals mark the document and force MFA (R-INJECTION); everything the reader returns carries `UNTRUSTED` until the user confirms it, and tainted payees cannot be paid during the hold (INV-4).
- **Evidence.** Run D uses the poisoned housing PDF (`rt_housing_deposit.pdf`): the reader flags `payment_instruction_unmatched` and `injection_suspected`, the proposal needs MFA, the payee is saved on hold and the payment is denied.

### 4.5 Published results (report `20261011T175100`)

- **Report id:** `20261011T175100` (`eval/reports/20261011T175100/report.md` and `report.json`). **Seeds: 1.** Planner provider: cloud; 30 cloud calls plus recorded replay. Vintage: an earlier build (11 October 2026), before the risk score and later changes.
- **Scope in that report:** 11 tasks, 72 attack cases and 10 controls per simulated user. This is a historical one-seed preview. **The expanded suite (68 tasks, 125 red-team cases) has not been evaluated**: the clean run is pending because the cloud planner is quota-blocked, and the ablation rows in that report are marked STALE. The interrupted later evaluation attempt contains infrastructure errors and is not a result.
- **Fooled user** (confirms everything and supplies valid TOTP codes): baseline moved NPR 1,018,730.00 with 7 harmful outcomes; Corridor AI full moved NPR 0.00 with 0 harmful outcomes, 0 data-extraction leaks, 0 duplicate payments, 100.0% numeric grounding and 100.0% task completion (11 of 11). The full system's case-level attack success rate was 8.3%: those are the 7 attacker payees that were saved but held for 24 hours (outcome "contained", no money moved). By category the full system showed indirect injection by document 22.2% (n=9), by image 10.0% (n=10) and induced transfer 20.0% (n=15), all of the held-payee kind; every other category was 0.0%.
- **Cautious user** (confirms only what matches the request): baseline moved NPR 1,372,847.45 with 10 harmful outcomes; Corridor AI full moved NPR 0.00, with attack success rate 0.0%.
- One seed means no confidence interval. These numbers identify the shape of the result; they are not a final performance claim.

## 7. Production hardening roadmap

1. A clean evaluation of the frozen code, several seeds, ablations re-run, infrastructure errors disclosed.
2. Independent penetration test and cryptographic audit; fix findings before any real money.
3. Real identity: replace the persona gateway with the bank's login; per-device MFA enrolment; officer SSO with role separation and four-eyes approval for payee verification.
4. Durable proposal and session state; protect or drop the conversation checkpoint of typed text.
5. Sign Merkle roots and store them outside the database; add Credit Passport revocation and a standard SD-JWT VC with managed keys.
6. A self-hosted or bank-contracted inference endpoint; no third-party retention.
7. Tune the policy for new payees (lower daily limit, bank scam warnings); review all limits with the bank's risk team.
8. Native review of every translated string; accessibility audit.
